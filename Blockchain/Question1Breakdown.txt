PART 1 — Architecture Choice
8 Marks
The question is:

"Should UoM use a public permissionless blockchain or a private permissioned blockchain?"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You have two options.

Option A — Public Permissionless Blockchain

Examples:

- Ethereum
- Polygon

Anyone can participate in the network.

There is no central organisation controlling who can operate nodes.

Advantages

1. High transparency
- The blockchain is publicly accessible.
- An employer anywhere in the world can independently inspect blockchain records.

2. Strong decentralisation\
- UoM doesn't control the entire network.
- Even if UoM's own servers disappear, the blockchain continues to exist.

3. Global verification
- An overseas employer or university doesn't have to trust UoM's database.
- They can independently verify the blockchain proof.

Disadvantages

1. Privacy concerns
- Putting student information directly onto a public blockchain would be problematic.
- Blockchain data is generally visible to anyone.
- Student information could potentially remain publicly accessible permanently.

2. Gas/transaction fees
- Transactions on public blockchains require fees.
- These costs may fluctuate.
- For a university issuing thousands of degrees, transaction costs need to be considered.

3. Less institutional control
- UoM cannot control the underlying Ethereum/Polygon network.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Option B — Private Permissioned Blockchain

Example:

Hyperledger Fabric

Here, only authorised organisations can participate.

For example:

             UoM
              |
       ----------------
       |              |
      UTM          Open University
       |              |
       ----------------
              |
        Consortium
        Blockchain



Potential consortium members could include:

University of Mauritius
University of Technology, Mauritius
Open University of Mauritius
potentially relevant government/HEC institutions
Advantages

1. Better privacy
- Only authorised participants can access the network.
- This makes it much more suitable for educational records.

2. No traditional public gas fees
- The university doesn't need to pay Ethereum/Polygon-style gas fees for every transaction.
- Instead, it has infrastructure/node operating costs.

3. Governance
- The participating universities can collectively govern the network.
For example:

UoM cannot simply modify the blockchain by itself.

Disadvantages

1. Infrastructure cost
- Universities have to maintain nodes, servers, networking and security.

2. Less decentralised than Ethereum
- There are fewer participants.
- If all nodes are controlled by a small consortium, decentralisation is lower.

3. Global verification
- A foreign employer may not automatically know or trust a Mauritius consortium blockchain.
- You therefore need to design a mechanism allowing external parties to verify the proof easily.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What does the examiner want here?

Don't simply write:

"Hyperledger Fabric is better because it is private."

That would be weak.

You need to compare the two architectures against the three criteria given in the question.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Criterion 1 — Privacy

Ask:

Where should student information exist?

You should argue that personal information should NOT be stored directly on a public blockchain.

For example, don't put:

{Name: Yohann Babbea
Student ID: XXXXX
Degree: BSc FinTech
Date of Birth: XX/XX/XXXX}

directly on Ethereum.
Instead:

{OFF-CHAIN
Student information
        ↓
Secure UoM database
        ↓
Cryptographic hash
        ↓
ON-CHAIN
Hash only}

This becomes very important in Part 3.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Criterion 2 — Cost

You need to think in Mauritian Rupees (MUR).

You don't necessarily need to calculate an exact price unless your lecturer specifically expects it.

You should discuss:

Public blockchain

Costs include:

transaction/gas fees
potentially fluctuating fees
wallet/key management
smart contract deployment
Permissioned blockchain

Costs include:

servers/nodes
maintenance
network infrastructure
cybersecurity
software administration

So you're effectively comparing:

transaction fees vs infrastructure/maintenance costs

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Criterion 3 — Trust & Verifiability

This is probably one of the most important concepts in the assignment.

The problem says:

Employers shouldn't have to rely on UoM's IT servers remaining online forever.

Imagine UoM's website/database disappears in 30 years.

Can someone still verify that:

"This degree was genuinely issued by UoM in 2026"?

That's the purpose of blockchain.

Likely recommendation

A strong answer could recommend:

A private permissioned consortium blockchain using Hyperledger Fabric, combined with off-chain storage and cryptographic hashes.

Why?

Because the system involves sensitive educational records, making privacy and controlled access extremely important.

However, you must address the weakness:

"How can an overseas employer verify it without contacting UoM?"

Your architecture can allow public verification of cryptographic proofs, digital credentials, QR codes, or a verification portal/API without exposing the underlying student database.
