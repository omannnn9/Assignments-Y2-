Oman - Workflow: Degree issuance and on-chain recording 

The process of degree issuance starts when a student successfully completes all requirements for their degree and the University's examination board approves the issuance for their degree. Once this is successful, the UoM examinations office generates a digital credential for the student. The credential contains important academic data such as name, student id, programme of study, date awarded, credential id and institution. 

To ensure authenticity and prevent risk of falsified documents, the credential is digitally signed using the private key of the university. The private key acts as the digital signature of the university and also as proof that it was issued by UoM. Any other institution or employer can later verify the authenticity the university's public key.

After the credential has been signed, it is hashed using the SHA-256 hash function. The hash generated acts as a unique id of the credential. Because of the deterministic and collision-resistant properties of SHA-256, even a small value modification to the credential would produce a completely different hash value. This allows tampering attempts to be easily detected.

##############
To protect student privacy and comply with data protection requirements, the full credential and personal student information are stored off chain in a secure UoM database. Information such as student names, IDs, transcripts and academic records are therefore not exposed on the blockchain.

Only a limited set of verification data is recorded on the permissioned Hyperledger Fabric consortium blockchain operated by participating institutions. The blockchain record contains the credential ID, the SHA-256 hash of the credential, the issuing institution, a timestamp and the credential status (valid or revoked).

The blockchain transaction is then submitted to the consortium network where authorised nodes validate the transaction and reach consensus. Once approved, the transaction is added to a new block and permanently recorded on the distributed ledger. This approach combines the privacy of off-chain storage with the security, transparency and tamper-resistance of blockchain technology, ensuring that academic credentials can be trusted and verified in the future.
