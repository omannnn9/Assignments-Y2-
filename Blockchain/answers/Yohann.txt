The proposed blockchain-based degree verification system must comply with the Data Protection Act 2017 (DPA 2017) of Mauritius. Academic credentials contain personal data such as a student's name, student ID, programme and qualification details. The DPA 2017 requires personal data to be processed lawfully, fairly and transparently. It also requires data to be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary, while respecting applicable rights of data subjects.

A major privacy concern is the immutability of blockchain records. Once information is recorded on a blockchain, it is designed to remain permanently available and resistant to alteration. This is beneficial for preventing fraudulent modification of degree records, but it can create difficulties when personal information needs to be corrected, erased or its processing restricted. Under Section 39 of the DPA 2017, individuals may have rights to rectification, erasure or restriction of processing in specific circumstances. Therefore, storing identifiable student information directly on an immutable blockchain could create tension with data protection requirements.

To address this issue, the proposed system should use an off-chain and on-chain approach. Personal and educational information should remain in a secure UoM database rather than being stored directly on the blockchain. This can include the student's name, student ID, programme and detailed qualification information. The blockchain should instead contain only the minimum information required for verification, such as the SHA-256 hash, a non-sensitive credential reference, issuing institution and timestamp. This approach follows the principle of data minimisation by avoiding unnecessary personal information on the blockchain.

When an employer or overseas institution verifies a degree, the stored credential information can be compared with the blockchain's cryptographic record to determine whether it has been altered. The blockchain therefore provides tamper-evident verification without unnecessarily exposing personal data. Meanwhile, information stored off-chain can be corrected, removed or managed according to applicable data protection and retention requirements.

Overall, separating personal data from the immutable blockchain allows UoM to balance security, fraud prevention and verifiability with privacy and compliance under the DPA 2017.
