To issue digital tamper proof academic degrees using blockchain technology, the UoM can consider the architecture from the choice of either a public permissionless blockchain or a private permissioned blockchain. 
A public blockchain is a fully open, decentralized, and permissionless digital ledger system which operates on a peer-to-peer network where anyone can join without needing approval from a central authority. These may include Polygon, Ethereum, Algorand, and Solana. These blockchains, can provide independent, global and resilient verification offering a long-term availability. Therefore, employers can verify the authenticity of a degree anywhere and any time, without dependency on UoM’s servers, since many network nodes maintain the ledger and thus the proof remains available even if UoM’s portal is temporarily offline. Furthermore, this can lower operational burden and widen interoperability, meaning that UoM can use an existing worldwide network instead of running a consortium of institutional nodes through public-chain standards, QR-code verification, and digital-credential tools to make the degree easier to use internationally, which is much cost-effective. 
However, the overriding concerns can be about the privacy, irreversibility, public visibility and possible linking of hashes. Records on public blockchain are difficult to delete. If UoM placed names, student Ids, grades, or certificates on chain, it could prevent student’s right to erasure and conflict with the Mauritius Data Protection Act (DPA) 2017 which protects individuals’ personal data and authorizes how organizations use it. Additionally, records require fees in cryptocurrency, which are volatile and thus UoM may not always predict the cost in MUR. Therefore if a public network is busy, transactions may cost more, leading to uncertain gas fees and network congestion thus being unfavourable for UoM.
