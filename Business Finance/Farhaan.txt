To finance the proposed acquisition of TechWave Ltd, IslandTech Ltd must carefully evaluate three primary funding options: debt financing, equity financing, or using internal funds through retained earnings. Each method carries distinct financial and operational risks that the board needs to balance. Borrowing money through loans or bonds increases IslandTech’s financial leverage. Although interest payments provide a tax shield, debt creates fixed interest and capital repayment obligations that must be met regardless of performance. If the expected cash flows from TechWave fail to materialize, these high debt commitments significantly increase the risk of financial distress or default. Lenders may also impose strict debt covenants that restrict future operational flexibility.

Alternatively, issuing new equity avoids immediate cash drain and fixed interest burdens. However, creating new shares dilutes the ownership percentage of existing shareholders, which can lower Earnings Per Share (EPS) and alter voting control unless pre-emptive rights are exercised. Lastly, using internal funds avoids both debt servicing costs and share dilution, but it carries a significant opportunity cost by depleting cash reserves that could otherwise be used for dividend payouts or emergency liquidity.

A key argument put forward by some directors is that acquiring TechWave will automatically benefit shareholders simply because IslandTech will become a larger company with a higher market share. However, in corporate finance, an increase in company size or market share does not automatically translate into shareholder wealth creation. Wealth creation occurs only when an investment generates a Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) that exceeds the company's Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC), resulting in a positive Net Present Value (NPV).

Pursuing growth for its own sake can actually destroy value. As companies expand, they often encounter diseconomies of scale, where management complexity and bureaucratic overheads increase, leading to operational inefficiencies that reduce overall profitability. Furthermore, gaining market share frequently requires price reductions or high promotional spending, which inflates top-line revenue while shrinking profit margins. Therefore, IslandTech must ensure that TechWave contributes real economic value and profit rather than simply expanding the firm's physical size.

To ensure that the acquisition of TechWave Ltd creates long-term value, IslandTech’s management must properly align its dividend decisions with its growth strategy. If earnings are retained to fund the acquisition, management must clearly communicate the strategic benefits to shareholders to avoid negative dividend signalling risks. Furthermore, IslandTech must establish a strict valuation approach to avoid paying an excessively high acquisition premium, ensuring that expected synergies are realistic and achievable. Finally, adopting a balanced hybrid financing approach that combines moderate retained earnings with manageable debt can help fund the acquisition without over-leveraging the firm or causing excessive equity dilution.
