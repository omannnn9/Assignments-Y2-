An acquisition will occur when the acquirer (IslandTech) purchases a controlling interest in a targeted company (TechWave). This type of investment is normally done with motives to be more profitable. In the case of IslandTech, the Board is expecting the acquisition to increase shareholder wealth because IslandTech will become larger, and therefore gain other benefits as well.

These motives can include, firstly, the possible economies of scale. This means that with a larger combined entity, IslandTech can diversify its fixed costs, such as research and development, IT infrastructure, and marketing, through a bigger output. This can therefore provide ISlandTech with a greater bargaining power with suppliers to negotiate for discounts which can further reduce the average cost per unit. Since TechWave is a direct competitor, considering it to be a horizontal acquisition will probably make the scale benefits to be plausible. However, IslandTech can still face diseconomies of scale when operation goes beyond a certain point since economies are not unlimited, which can slow decision-making and reduce coordination.

Secondly, another gain can be in terms of an in increase in market share and market power. Taking over TechWave as a competitor, removes a rival for IslandTech and therefore can enlarge its market share. This reduced competitive pressure can provide IslandTech with a stronger pricing power, which can create some barriers for new entrants to compete in the market, therefore this validates the Board’s goal of strengthening the company’s position. However, since the two companies are direct competitors, having influence over prices, quality, and choices available for customers, competition authorities can intervene by delaying, blocking or imposing conditions on the deal (for eg laws from the Commission of Mauritius, competition Act 2007), thus reducing the benefits gained.
